How to read this

A computer watched every pitch from two camera angles and tracked only the pitcher's body: head, shoulders, elbows, hands, glove, hips, knees and feet. It never sees the scoreboard, the catcher or the batter. It then tried to name the pitch type from his body alone, before release, on games it had never seen.
  • Does he tip? One line per angle and delivery. Clear means a hitter who knows what to look for could use it. Weak means a small but real edge. No tell means his body gave nothing away before release.
  • Games. Tap a game to see the pitches where his body gave the type away, with the clip and a typical pitch of another type beside it.
  • Every number is tested against guessing. "Usual pitch" is what you get by always guessing his most common pitch. "Count only" is what the count, outs and runners give with no video. A tell has to beat both.
  • Fix. Each tell comes with what to change, and each game page has a quick fix under its pitches.
